Using AI ethically at school

Learn how to use AI honestly, protect privacy, question bias, and disclose your use at school.

Academic integrity

Academic integrity means being honest about your work and following the rules for an assignment. Getting help is not automatically cheating: a teacher might allow you to ask an AI tool for a practice question or a hint, then solve the assigned problem yourself. But submitting AI-written work as if you wrote it can misrepresent who did the work and may violate the assignment rules.

Rules vary by class and task. For example, College Board's AP Capstone guidance permits certain uses, such as exploring topics or checking grammar, while setting limits on writing and rewriting. Do not assume that permission in one class applies everywhere. Read the instructions and ask your teacher if you are unsure.

When help is permitted, use it to support your thinking. Take notes, make your own decisions, and be ready to explain your work. Keep drafts or a record of your process if your teacher requests them. Acknowledging AI use does not make a prohibited use acceptable.

Privacy

Before you paste anything into a chatbot, pause and consider whose information it contains. Do not share passwords, account numbers, home addresses, phone numbers, medical details, or other sensitive information about yourself or someone else. Avoid classmates' names and private stories, too. Even a school assignment may contain personal details that its author did not expect you to share.

Instead, describe the problem without identifying anyone: “A student is having trouble organizing a schedule” is safer than including a name and personal history. Remove identifying details from examples, and ask your teacher before uploading class materials. Some details can still identify a person even after a name is removed, so check the whole passage.

Services can handle and store data in different ways. UNESCO's guidance stresses protecting learners' data privacy; read the tool's privacy information and follow school rules before using it. If you accidentally share sensitive information, tell a trusted adult or teacher promptly and follow their guidance.

Make this prompt safe

1. Select the private parts of this fictional draft.

“Help me write a message to my about how to after she missed class for .”

2. What should you do before using AI to draft the message?

3. Use a safer prompt if AI is allowed

“Help me draft a message to a lab partner about how to catch up on a missed lab.”

Example disclosure. “I used an AI chatbot to help draft this message; I removed personal details first.”

Bias and fairness

Bias is an unfair pattern in how people or groups are represented or treated. An AI tool can repeat or amplify bias found in the information used to build it or in the way it is designed and used. UNESCO's guidance on AI in education discusses risks involving bias and representation.

For example, imagine asking an image tool to show scientists and receiving pictures of people who all look alike. That result does not prove what scientists look like; it may leave many people out. Similarly, a chatbot might describe a group using a stereotype, a fixed and oversimplified idea about its members.

When you notice a possible problem, question the output and compare it with reliable sources and a range of perspectives. You can revise the prompt to request broader representation, but a new answer still needs review. Avoid using an AI response alone to judge a person's abilities or make a decision about them. Ask a teacher for help evaluating questionable content.

Citing and disclosing AI use

Citing identifies a source you used; disclosing explains how you used a tool. Both help a reader understand your work. Start with your teacher's instructions: some assignments prohibit AI, while others allow it with a citation or a note. A disclosure cannot give you permission to use AI when the assignment forbids it.

If your teacher allows AI for brainstorming, a disclosure could say: “I used ChatGPT to brainstorm possible topics, then chose my topic and wrote this essay myself.” Adjust the sentence to describe what you actually did and to name the tool you used. If required, include details such as the prompt, model, date, or a shareable conversation link, provided it contains no private information.

The MLA Style Center provides guidance for citing generated content and recommends saying so when AI did a meaningful part of the work. Check the required citation style and your teacher's directions. Verify sources the AI suggests, read them yourself, and cite the sources you actually use.
