Try AI yourself

Three small demos you can play with right now: split text into tokens, predict the next word, and build a prompt that is easy for an AI to follow.

Nothing you type on this page leaves your browser. All three demos are plain JavaScript running on this page: there is no internet request, no account, and no API key, and your text is not saved.

1 · How text becomes tokens

A language model does not read letters the way you do. Before it can work, text is cut into tokens: sometimes a whole word, sometimes a piece of a long word, and sometimes a punctuation mark. Each token is then turned into numbers the model can calculate with.

    • word whole word
    • piec piece of a long word (up to 4 characters)
    • , punctuation

    This is a simplified illustration, not a real tokenizer. A real model uses a learned vocabulary of many thousands of pieces, and it also has its own rules for spaces, accents, and emoji, so your count here will not match a real model.

    2 · Guess the next word

    Language models generate text by repeatedly predicting what is likely to come next, and the prediction is based on patterns in their training text. This demo is a tiny version: it counts which word follows each word in one short paragraph, then reuses those counts. It can only ever produce words it has already seen, which is exactly why a real model needs an enormous amount of text.

    The "generate" button picks each next word by weighted random: a word seen twice as often in the paragraph is twice as likely to be chosen. If a run reaches the end of a sentence, it starts again from another word, shown as … . That is how a language model produces different answers to the same question, in a very small way.

    3 · Build a stronger prompt

    A prompt works better when it says who the AI should act as, what you actually want, the context it needs, and how the answer should be laid out. When a part is missing, the model has to guess, and it usually fills the gap with something generic. Fill in the fields below and watch the prompt and the checklist change.

      Weak prompt vs strong prompt

      Weak prompt

      Write about photosynthesis.

      The AI has to guess the audience, the length, and the purpose. The answer may be too long, too simple, or aimed at the wrong reader.

      Strong prompt

      Role: You are a patient science tutor for a Year 10 student. Task: Explain photosynthesis in three short paragraphs. Context: I have a test on Friday and the chemical equation confuses me. Format: Three paragraphs, then a two-line summary.

      Every part is stated, so the AI can aim the explanation at the right reader and stop at the right length.
